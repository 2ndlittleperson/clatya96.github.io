CARDS: Comprehensive AR Docent System

An augmented reality docent that guides a visitor through one artwork the way a human guide would — in sequence, at the right depth, with the right comparison — using nothing but where the visitor stands and which way they turn.

Role
Co-first author Three-person team: Seung Un Lee, Jiyoung Yun, Dain Kim
Context
KAIST CTP445, Augmented Reality Course project, later extended for publication
Published

The system

Why we built it

Augmented reality has been generous to exhibitions on the spectacle side: paintings that animate, figures that step out of their frames, galleries that rebuild themselves around you. The educational side has had much less attention. A visitor with a mobile guide is usually handed the same block of text as everyone else, in the same order, no matter where they are standing or what part of the canvas they are actually looking at.

CARDS asks what happens if the docent’s job — deciding what to point at, how deep to go, and what to compare the work with — is the thing the AR is designed around. The result is a guide that reads the visitor’s position and orientation as input, so moving through the room is the interface. Nothing is buried in a menu, and attention stays on the artwork rather than on the phone.

Three interactions

01

Localized guides with AR cues

Numbered pins sit on the parts of the canvas a trained guide would point to, in the order a guide would use them, so the sequence carries the reading rather than a paragraph of text.

  • Pins change colour once they have been opened, so what is left to see is legible at a glance.
  • Visitors can like individual pins; the totals roll up into a single heart indicator above the work, making collective attention visible.

02

Layer change by distance

Standing back gives the wide reading — subject, period, why the work matters. Walking closer swaps the overlay for progressively finer detail, down to brushwork and iconography.

  • Scope maps onto physical distance, so general-to-specific needs no control to operate and no instruction to understand.
  • Pairing distance-triggered layers with the sequential pins is the combination the paper puts forward as new.

03

Reference works by rotation angle

Turning away from the painting by a few degrees brings a related work into view beside it, instead of sending the visitor off to a search screen and back.

  • Comparison happens inside one continuous experience, so the visitor never loses their place in the guide.
  • The gesture is the idea made literal: looking at a work from a different angle is what shows you a different picture.

What it argues

Taken together the three interactions treat the visitor’s body as the control surface: position sets depth, orientation sets breadth, and the pin sequence sets order. That is a different proposition from an AR guide that simply overlays the same audio tour on a canvas, and it is the part of the work the ISMAR submission argues for.
